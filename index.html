Step 3

Fill it in: your 1-6-4

Put the big rocks down first. Work always fills in around them — but only if you protect the good stuff up front.

1 · Misogi

A year-defining event. Choose one challenging goal that sets the tone for the year. Go BIG! A physical challenge, a trip, a business goal, something on your bucket list.

Stuck on yours? It doesn't have to be a race — write a book, learn piano, hit a number in your business. Pick something that scares you a little. Then whatever you're thinking, double it.

6 · Mini adventures

We call it Kevin's Rule: every other month, do something out of your routine — a hike, a polar plunge, a cooking class, a weekend trip. Six a year you wouldn't have done otherwise. In 30 years, that's 180 new experiences!

It doesn't take money: a polar plunge is a subway token; Mount Washington was "$18 to park."

4 · Winning habits

It takes about 90 days to lock in a habit, so add just one each quarter and watch them compound. Write it down, check it off daily.

Need ideas? Try the 3 C's — compliment, congratulate, or console someone every day by text, email, or call. Or 100 oz of water, a 10-minute meditation, 10k steps.

Step 4

Keep it going

No rigid daily script — just enough rhythm to stay on offense.

Plan the week on Sunday

Sunday night, pull the week onto paper — a view you can see at a glance and move around. This is where the planner sits next to the calendar.

The Big A## Planner →

Take your vitamins

List the 10 things that make a day good — a run, a sauna, a walk with your partner, time with the kids. You can't do all 10, so take two or three a day.

Three hours for you

Those vitamins, added up — cumulative, not one block. 20 minutes here, a walk there. You don't have to be rich to be time rich, just organized enough to prioritize yourself.

When December comes

Close out your year

Every business closes its books and runs a review. Do it for your life — a couple of hours so you start the new year light, not playing catch-up.

Get light

  • Closet — 50/50? Donate it
  • Desk — clear the clutter
  • Email — respond, file, delete to zero
  • Unsubscribe + dead apps
  • Car, files, bills — current

The blender test

Drop every bucket of your life in a blender — health, finances, relationships, work, where you live, fun — and rate the whole thing 1–10. The two or three that drag the number down? Those are your real fixes for next year. Not twenty resolutions.

Write your thank-you notes

Close the year by thanking 20–30 people who showed up for you. Everybody reads their mail — it's an ink stain in the brain.
